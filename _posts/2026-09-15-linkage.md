* There's so much going on at once in the LLM/mathematics world and so many people providing insightful comment on it that I'm not even going to try to add to it <span style="white-space:nowrap">([$$\mathbb{M}$$](https://mathstodon.xyz/@11011110/117245434691089974)).</span> Instead, here's a nice recent preprint where "all original results, proof strategies, and mathematical content predate the use of AI assistance" despite some AI use in cleaning up the writeup: "[Forbidden subgraphs of graphs with low bandwidth](https://
arxiv.org/abs/2609.01949)", by Maria Chudnovsky, Daniel Lokshtanov, and Eran Nevo. Given any <span style="white-space:nowrap">graph $$G$$,</span> in fixed-parameter time in a <span style="white-space:nowrap">parameter $$k$$,</span> they find either a subtree of <span style="white-space:nowrap">bandwidth $$\ge k$$</span> or a linear layout of <span style="white-space:nowrap">bandwidth $$\le f(k)$$</span> for some (badly exponential) <span style="white-space:nowrap">function $$f$$.</span> There is additionally a structure theorem describing the high-bandwidth subtrees as having a special form (high pathwidth, or high ratio of vertices to diameter, or a "skewed Cantor comb".
