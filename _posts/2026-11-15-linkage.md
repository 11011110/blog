* I made this illustration <span style="white-space:nowrap">([$$\mathbb{M}$$](https://mathstodon.xyz/@11011110/117590642760647157))</span> for [the Wikipedia article on the problem of folding a square to make a shape of greater perimeter than you started with](https://
en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napkin_folding_problem). It depicts a map from a square to a tree, that does not increase any distance (a "short map"), but that does increase the perimeter of its image. Each red circle or blue circular arc in the square is mapped to its thick centerline at the point where it crosses the centerline; these centerlines become the branches of the tree. The yellow regions of the square are all mapped to a single point where the branches meet. The perimeter of the black part of the tree is the same as the square; the doubled length of the red branch is added on top of that. Svetlana Krat's 2005 Ph.D. thesis includes both this map (and its generalization to rectangles) and a method of approximating short maps by foldings, to give a novel solution to the napkin folding problem.
